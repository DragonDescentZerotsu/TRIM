# Practical Interpretation Manual for Molecular Properties in Skin_Reaction

## neutral fraction
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): Using the reference pH as the basis, when pH = pKa the distribution is about 50%/50%; when they differ by 1 pH unit it is about 90%/10%; when they differ by 2 pH units it is about 99%/1%. For skin-exposure proxies, a higher neutral fraction usually favors passive skin permeation; however, there is no stable standalone threshold for skin sensitization itself.
- Usually associated with: A higher neutral fraction usually means a greater chance of entering the stratum corneum; if reactivity alerts are also present, it more readily supports B.
- Brief note: This is a proxy for “skin accessibility,” not “sensitization reactivity” itself; this task is closer to skin sensitization than to simple skin permeation.
- Source: See the task description and literature on pH partitioning, skin barrier pH, and skin permeation.

## estimated logD
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): In neighboring proxy tasks such as passive skin permeation or transdermal delivery, the common empirical range is about 1–3; a broader empirical window can be extended to 0–5. However, there is no stable hard cutoff for skin sensitization, and logKow/logD ≤ 1 cannot be used as an exclusion rule for “non-sensitizers.”
- Usually associated with: Moderate logD is more commonly seen in molecules with skin-entry capability; values that are too low are often too hydrophilic, while values that are too high may cause retention in the stratum corneum, so overall flux may not be higher.
- Brief note: If logD is to be used in this task, it is better interpreted at an effective skin barrier pH of about 5.7–6.3 (average about 6), rather than by mechanically applying logP.
- Source: See reviews on skin permeation, studies on effective skin pH, and analyses refuting fixed “logKow thresholds” for skin sensitization.

## strongest acidic pKa
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found; in practice, interpret relative to the reference pH: if the acidic site's pKa is about 2 units above the environmental pH, it is mostly neutral HA; if it is about 2 units below, it is mostly ionized A−.
- Usually associated with: When other conditions are similar, acids that remain neutral more easily are usually more favorable for skin entry; however, this is not a standalone decision rule for skin sensitization.
- Brief note: This property mainly affects exposure accessibility through neutral fraction and logD.
- Source: See Henderson–Hasselbalch and skin pH-partition literature.

## strongest basic pKa
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found; in practice, interpret relative to the reference pH: if the basic site's pKa is about 2 units below the environmental pH, it is mostly neutral B; if it is about 2 units above, it is mostly positively charged BH+.
- Usually associated with: Weak bases that more easily remain neutral are usually more favorable for passive skin permeation; strongly basic compounds that remain positively charged for long periods are often less favorable for skin entry.
- Brief note: This is still a proxy for “exposure accessibility” and should not be treated as a standalone hard rule for skin sensitization.
- Source: See Henderson–Hasselbalch and skin pH-partition literature.

## number of acidic sites
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: More acidic sites usually lower the neutral fraction and reduce passive skin permeation; considered alone, they cannot reliably distinguish A from B.
- Brief note: In this task, reactivity alerts and protein covalent binding potential are usually more important than the “number of sites.”
- Source: See skin sensitization QSAR/AOP and skin permeation proxy literature.

## number of basic sites
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: More basic sites usually increase the ionized fraction and reduce passive skin permeation; considered alone, they cannot reliably distinguish A from B.
- Brief note: Skin sensitization depends more on reaction chemistry than on any universal cutoff for the number of basic sites.
- Source: See mechanistic/QSAR reviews of skin sensitization and literature on skin pH.

## number of ionizable sites
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: More ionizable sites in total usually make it harder to maintain a high neutral fraction and thus less favorable for passive skin permeation.
- Brief note: This is a proxy for “getting into skin,” not for “covalent reaction with proteins.”
- Source: See literature on skin pH, logD, and the relationship between skin sensitization and skin permeation.

## exact molecular weight
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): An older rule of thumb often uses <500 Da as a heuristic for “skin permeable”; a stricter simplified skin permeation rule treats ≤152 Da as a “good penetrant” and >213 Da as a “bad penetrant.” However, in skin sensitization, >500 Da is not a reliable exclusion rule.
- Usually associated with: Lower molecular weight more easily supports skin entry; however, MW alone cannot reliably determine A or B.
- Brief note: The Skin_Reaction data are essentially derived from skin sensitization/rLLNA, so the “500 Da rule” should not be treated as a labeling threshold for this task.
- Source: See task provenance, literature challenging the 500 Da rule, and simplified skin permeation rules.

## fraction of sp3 carbons
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: In skin sensitization, fraction sp3 does not have practical empirical anchors comparable to reactivity structural alerts or logP/MW.
- Source: See mechanistic/QSAR reviews of skin sensitization.

## heavy-atom count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: As a proxy for molecular size, a larger heavy-atom count is usually less favorable for passive skin permeation; thus, with other factors held equal, it is often less favorable for the exposure accessibility of B.
- Brief note: However, there is no universal cutoff for skin sensitization itself.
- Source: See skin permeation models and skin sensitization reviews.

## heavy-atom molecular weight
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found; in practice it can be treated approximately like MW as a size proxy.
- Usually associated with: Higher values usually indicate larger molecules that are harder to permeate the skin passively.
- Brief note: At the task level, there is no “skin sensitization threshold specific to heavy-atom molecular weight.”
- Source: See classical skin permeability models and skin sensitization reviews.

## Labute surface area
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: As a size/surface-area proxy, larger values are usually unfavorable for passive skin permeation.
- Brief note: The skin sensitization literature does not provide a stable discriminative cutoff for Labute surface area.
- Source: See skin permeation models and skin sensitization reviews.

## maximum absolute partial charge
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: If this high local charge is located near the reactive center, it often suggests stronger electronic polarization/reactivity and more readily supports B; however, there is no universal cutoff across chemical domains.
- Brief note: These electronic descriptors are more suitable within specific mechanistic domains than as global one-size-fits-all rules.
- Source: See quantum chemistry/QSAR literature on skin sensitization.

## maximum partial charge
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: A more positive local atomic charge at an electrophilic center usually better supports protein addition reactions, and thus tends to favor B.
- Brief note: It is more meaningful when compared only within the same reaction mechanism domain.
- Source: See quantum chemistry/QSAR literature on skin sensitization.

## minimum absolute partial charge
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No universal one-way threshold.
- Brief note: For skin sensitization, the most actionable features are still structural alerts and reaction mechanisms rather than this single global value.
- Source: See mechanistic/QSAR reviews of skin sensitization.

## minimum partial charge
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: For a small number of “nucleophilic sensitizers,” more electron-rich sites may have mechanistic significance; however, there is no universal cutoff.
- Brief note: This is a newer mechanistic perspective and still should not be used as a single threshold across all chemical space.
- Source: See literature on nucleophilic sensitizers and mechanistic domains.

## estimated logP
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): For skin-entry proxies, the most common empirical range is about 1–3 (sometimes written as 1–4); however, for skin sensitization there is no stable threshold such as “logP > 1 is required for sensitization.” In some logP-dependent electrophile domains, the maximum effect may occur around logP 5.5, but this is not a global rule.
- Usually associated with: Moderate lipophilicity more often supports skin entry; very low logP is unfavorable for skin entry, while very high logP may increase retention in the stratum corneum rather than overall flux.
- Brief note: Mechanistic QMM for skin sensitization explicitly models hydrophobicity (often represented by logP) together with reactivity; logP alone is not enough.
- Source: See skin permeation reviews, mechanistic QMM reviews, and literature refuting fixed “logKow thresholds.”

## molecular weight
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): An old heuristic uses <500 Da; a more flux-oriented simplified rule classifies ≤152 Da as tending toward good penetrants and >213 Da as tending toward bad penetrants. However, in skin sensitization, 500 Da is not a reliable negative threshold.
- Usually associated with: Lower MW more often supports skin entry; if electrophilic alerts are also present, it more readily supports B.
- Brief note: “MW > 500” should not be directly mapped to A.
- Source: See task provenance, literature debating the 500 Da rule, and skin permeation rules.

## NH/OH group count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found; non-task-specific transdermal/non-oral proxies often favor fewer H-bond donors.
- Usually associated with: More NH/OH groups usually mean stronger hydrogen-bond donation, which is less favorable for passive skin permeation.
- Brief note: For skin sensitization, NH/OH is more like an “accessibility/polarity” proxy than a decisive mechanistic alert.
- Source: See non-oral Rule-of-5 proxies and literature on hydrogen bonding in skin permeability.

## nitrogen/oxygen atom count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: More N/O atoms usually mean higher polarity and greater hydrogen-bonding ability, which is less favorable for passive skin permeation.
- Brief note: But the count itself is not equivalent to sensitization reactivity.
- Source: See hydrogen-bond/size models for skin permeation and mechanistic literature on skin sensitization.

## aliphatic carbocycle count

- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: Whether a compound is sensitizing depends on whether the ring carries an alerting electrophile or an oxidizable site, not on the ring count itself.
- Source: See literature on structural alerts and autoxidation in skin sensitization.

- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: Heterocycles matter more in this task only when they introduce a reactive center.
- Source: See literature on structural alerts in skin sensitization.

## aliphatic ring count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: Ring count itself is far less important than reactivity structural alerts.
- Source: See mechanistic/structural alert reviews of skin sensitization.

## aromatic carbocycle count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: Aromatic rings are common in sensitizers such as aldehydes, quinones, and SNAr-activated aromatic systems, but the “number of aromatic rings” is not a universal threshold.

## aromatic heterocycle count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: It becomes more relevant only when the heteroaromatic ring participates in SNAr, oxidative activation, or carries an electrophile.
- Source: See literature on structural alerts in skin sensitization.

## aromatic ring count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: In this task, “what reactive group is attached to the aromatic ring” matters much more than “how many aromatic rings there are.”
- Source: See literature on structural alerts/mechanisms in skin sensitization.

## hydrogen-bond acceptor count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable task-specific threshold found; nearby non-oral/transdermal proxies often adopt HBA ≤10 as a loose upper bound, but this is not a rule specific to skin sensitization.
- Usually associated with: Lower HBA usually favors passive skin permeation.
- Brief note: If used, it is better treated as a “polarity proxy” rather than a skin sensitization threshold.
- Source: See non-oral Rule-of-5 proxies and descriptor literature for skin permeation.

## hydrogen-bond donor count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable task-specific threshold found; nearby non-oral/transdermal proxies often adopt HBD ≤5 as a loose upper bound.
- Usually associated with: Lower HBD usually favors passive skin permeation; higher HBD often reduces diffusion/partitioning.
- Brief note: HBD is often more disadvantageous than HBA for transdermal permeation, but there is no universal cutoff for skin sensitization hazard itself.
- Source: See non-oral Rule-of-5 proxies and literature on hydrogen bonding in skin permeability.

## heteroatom count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: More heteroatoms usually mean higher polarity and stronger hydrogen bonding, tending to reduce passive skin permeation.
- Brief note: However, heteroatoms themselves may also be part of alerting groups, so the direction should not be interpreted mechanically.
- Source: See skin permeation models and literature on structural alerts in skin sensitization.

## rotatable-bond count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable skin-sensitization threshold found; nearby proxy tasks often use a loose rule of ≤10, but skin literature suggests intestinal rules cannot simply be transferred to the epidermis.
- Usually associated with: Greater flexibility is usually less favorable for passive skin permeation.
- Brief note: In this task, it can serve as a weak proxy for “exposure accessibility” rather than for hazard itself.
- Source: See Veber/non-oral proxies and studies on epidermal permeation.

## saturated carbocycle count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: Whether the system is saturated is less important than whether it can form a peroxide or electrophile.
- Source: See literature on structural alerts and autoxidation in skin sensitization.

## saturated heterocycle count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.

- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: Ring count and saturation are both less important than the specific reactive functional group.
- Source: See mechanistic/structural alert reviews of skin sensitization.

## ring count
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way association.
- Brief note: Total ring count is usually not suitable as a criterion for this task.

## topological polar surface area
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable task-specific threshold found; nearby oral/non-oral proxies often use ≤140 Å², but skin literature suggests intestinal PSA rules cannot be directly applied to the epidermis.
- Usually associated with: Lower TPSA usually favors passive skin permeation.
- Brief note: TPSA is often a useful descriptor in skin permeation modeling, but it is not a universal cutoff for skin sensitization.
- Source: See epidermal permeation studies, effective skin pH QSPeR, and descriptor analyses.

## QED drug-likeness
- Common threshold(s) or range(s): No stable literature threshold found.
- Usually associated with: No stable one-way mapping to A/B.
- Brief note: QED is a general drug-likeness score; for a reaction-chemistry task like skin sensitization, it is much less interpretable than structural alerts or reaction mechanisms.
- Source: See mechanistic/QSAR reviews of skin sensitization.

## Functional-group notes

- Group name: α,β-unsaturated carbonyls (Michael acceptors)
- Usually associated with: B
- Brief note: This is one of the most classic and repeatedly observed skin sensitization alerts; it can undergo Michael addition with nucleophilic sites on proteins.
- Source: See literature on mechanistic domains and structural alerts in skin sensitization.

- Group name: Aldehydes / imine formers (Schiff base formers)
- Usually associated with: B
- Brief note: Aldehydes can form Schiff bases with protein amino groups and are one of the classic sensitization mechanisms.
- Source: See literature on mechanistic domains and structural alerts in skin sensitization.

- Group name: Acyl transfer agents (such as acid anhydrides, acyl chlorides, isocyanates, and isothiocyanates)
- Usually associated with: B
- Brief note: These groups are usually highly reactive and have long been regarded as closely related to skin sensitization.
- Source: See literature on contact allergy chemistry and high-potency structural rules.

- Group name: SN2 electrophiles (such as epoxides, activated alkyl halides, and alkyl sulfonates)
- Usually associated with: B
- Brief note: This is a classical mechanistic domain; potency is often influenced by both reactivity and hydrophobicity.
- Source: See literature on mechanistic domains and kDPRA-QMM.

- Group name: SNAr-activated aromatic rings
- Usually associated with: B
- Brief note: Aromatic rings bearing strong electron-withdrawing substituents and capable of nucleophilic aromatic substitution represent a well-defined skin sensitization reaction domain.
- Source: See mechanistic domain literature.

- Group name: Organic peroxides / hydroperoxides
- Usually associated with: B
- Brief note: Hydroperoxides formed by autoxidation are repeatedly validated as important sensitizers; multiple studies report them as strong sensitizers, especially among oxidized terpene fragrance products.
- Source: See literature on organic peroxides and fragrance hydroperoxides.

- Group name: Quinones / quinone methides and related systems that can generate electrophiles through oxidation
- Usually associated with: B
- Brief note: These systems can enter reactive domains through oxidative activation and usually deserve high-priority attention.
- Source: See literature on contact allergy chemistry and structural alerts.

- Group name: Possible nucleophilic sensitizer groups
- Usually associated with: B
- Brief note: Updated mechanistic literature indicates that a small number of sensitizers are not classical electrophiles but may act as nucleophiles in sensitization; such cases are not fully covered by traditional electrophile-only alerts.
- Source: See updated literature on nucleophilic sensitizers.
